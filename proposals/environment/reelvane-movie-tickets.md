- **Primary domain (required):** Media & entertainment
- **Secondary domains (optional):** None

## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** A resettable movie-ticket website in which an agent finds a showing that fits the people going (format, language and captions, accessibility, age rating, time), picks seats on an auditorium map, applies the right ticket types, membership allowance and vouchers, previews the all-in price, buys, and later exchanges or refunds within published cutoffs, including after a theater-initiated change. The environment records which seats each order holds, which ticket type and discount each seat used, which allowance or voucher was consumed and where every refund went, so outcomes can be checked from the final state.

Initial workflows:

1. **Showtimes, formats and seat maps.** Search by film, theater, date and time window. Filter by format (standard, large-format, 3D, premium sound), language version (original, dubbed, subtitled), open captions, audio description and age rating. Film pages list runtime, rating and content advisories. Showtime cards show the auditorium, format surcharges and available ticket types. Each auditorium has an original seat map with row/seat IDs, aisles and adjacency, recliners, wheelchair spaces with companion seats, and motion seats with age and height limits. The GUI map and the tool observations read the same inventory, and every task-relevant attribute is discoverable on the page.
2. **Hold, ticket types and all-in preview.** Select explicit seats and acquire an expiring hold. Assign a ticket type per seat (adult, child, senior, student with an ID note, member). Apply membership allowance, vouchers and promo codes under printed stacking rules, split payment between a gift card and a card, and add concessions for a pickup window. The preview shows per-seat prices, surcharges, fees, discounts, taxes and the final total in cents. Confirmation rechecks the hold, inventory, prices, allowance and policy version. Failures (seat taken during the hold, expired hold, invalid code, declined payment with a verification step) leave no charge or ticket, and a repeated request ID returns the original receipt.
3. **Orders, tickets and admission.** Inspect orders, per-seat tickets and concession lines. Distinguish confirmed, ticket-ready, exchanged, refunded, partially refunded and used. Mobile tickets are released a fixed time before the showing. A platform-operated synthetic admission event checks the ticket, the current showtime and the age-rating rule; it does not simulate real barcodes. An order confirmation alone does not prove that a ticket is valid for the current showtime.
4. **Exchanges, refunds and theater changes.** Preview and confirm an exchange to another showtime (price difference, exchange fee, allowance reuse). Alternatively, refund all or some seats to the original payment or as account credit, as each published policy allows, until the cutoff before the showing. Theater-initiated events (cancelled showing, auditorium change with seat remapping, withdrawn film) post notices with options and deadlines. Requested, approved and paid refunds remain distinct, and duplicate requests cannot refund twice.
5. **Membership allowance and rewards.** A synthetic subscription plan grants a weekly allowance of standard showings, premium-format surcharges, a cap on reservations held at once and a printed no-show rule. Rewards points accrue and redeem at published rates. The member page shows remaining allowance, the reservations counted against it, the renewal date and the points history. Pausing or cancelling the plan is deferred.

Illustrative downstream task: a parent wants to take two children and their grandfather to an animated film. The grandfather needs open captions and a wheelchair space with a companion seat. The family has a membership for one adult and a voucher that cannot be combined with member pricing. Later, a theater change moves the showing to a smaller auditorium. The agent must pick a feasible showing, seats and ticket types, use the allowance and the voucher only where allowed, and then choose between the offered exchange and a refund. The environment checks the final seats, ticket types, allowance and voucher use, charges, refunds and Mail/Calendar effects. The executable personal story and model-difficulty evidence belong to a separate Task proposal.

Fandango's and AMC's public pages inform interaction boundaries only. Reelvane's rule set is frozen and printed on the site, and no claim of compatibility with any real cinema's policies is made. Related [Seatline #45](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/45) covers live-event tickets, person-to-person transfers and admission credentials. Reelvane adds recurring showtimes, formats, language and caption versions, age ratings, ticket types, membership allowances, concessions and showtime exchanges, and it excludes transfers and resale. A shared seat-map data shape with #45 can be discussed at M0.

## 3. Potential app connections

- **Internal personal apps (required):** Mail for order, ticket-ready, exchange, refund and theater-change notices; Calendar for the showing (start, expected end including the pre-show, theater address) through a calendar intent; Contacts for companions' names when the purchaser shares tickets or receipts. Local tests use synthetic inbox, calendar and contact fixtures.
- **Other apps and websites (required):** None required. A local payment and gift-card ledger supplies deterministic charges, declines and refunds. [Finance #6](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/6) is an optional later transaction link. Live showtime feeds, ticketing and payment systems are excluded.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** A browser GUI plus same-state MCP/API tools for search, showtime and seat inspection, holds, previews and confirmation, orders and tickets, exchange and refund previews and requests, and membership and rewards; every page action maps to one tool call. Setup, reset, snapshot and restore, fixture loading, clock advance, other-buyer seat sales, theater changes, ticket release, admission checks and refund posting are platform or scenario operations behind an operator token and are never linked from pages. Final method names and actor binding follow the adopted platform contract ([#12](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/12)).
- **State and lifecycle (required):** Three layers are kept separate: a versioned catalog (films, theaters, auditoriums, seat maps, showtimes, price tables, policies), the account (profile, membership, payment methods, gift-card balance, rewards) and isolated run state (holds, previews, orders, tickets, concession lines, exchanges, refunds, notices, applied-event cursor). Writes carry `expected_version` and `request_id`. Previews carry a fingerprint and expire on the virtual clock. Stale or conflicting writes fail without partial effects, and the losing side of a seat race gets a clear conflict. Snapshots keep original receipts; reset restores every mutable record, ledger entry and delivery, checked by a hash over the data.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Incoming events can sell or release seats, change a price table, release mobile tickets, cancel or move a showing (with seat remapping and an options deadline), withdraw a film, reset weekly allowances, record admission and post refunds. Example: `showtime.cancelled` for a real showtime ID marks the affected orders, offers an exchange or refund until a deadline and sends one Mail notice per order containing the order ID and page path. Notices use the Mail CloudEvents format from #12, and Calendar updates use the agreed intent. Scenarios decide timing; the app does not choose the user's response.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable film, theater, auditorium, seat, showtime, hold, order, ticket, concession, voucher, member, refund and notice IDs; exact cents and currency; RFC 3339 instants and IANA time zones; ratings and content advisories stored as data. Original SVG seat maps derive from the same topology as the tool observations. Poster art is original or verified public domain (see section 4). No personal credentials or real payment data are used.
